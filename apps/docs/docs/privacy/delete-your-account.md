**Settings → Delete my data**. It cannot be undone, and the second half of what
it does is the part people do not expect:

> This cannot be undone. Please read what it does and does not remove — the
> second part is the one that surprises people.

[Export your data](./export-your-data.md) first. The screen offers it as a step,
because after this there is nothing to export.

## What is removed

> Your name, photo, payment handle, country, language and notification settings.
> Your sign-in, so this account can no longer be opened. Your devices,
> notification history, purchases and anything the AI scanner recorded about
> your usage.

Your account is closed and nobody can sign into it again.

Two things outlive it, and both are covered below: your share of the shared
ledger, and — if you filled it in — the answer you typed into
[**Why are you leaving?**](#doing-it)

## What stays, and why

> The expenses and settlements in your shared groups remain, along with the
> notes, comments and receipt images on them, because they are also other
> people's records — they are what says who owes whom, and removing them would
> silently change somebody else's balance to settle a debt nobody paid. You
> become an unnamed former member in those groups. Your name is gone from them;
> your share of the dinner is not.

This is worth reading twice. A shared ledger is not yours alone. If your half of
a ₹2,000 dinner vanished when you left, the other person's balance would quietly
change by ₹1,000 in your favour — a debt cancelled by an account deletion rather
than by a payment. So the amounts stay and your identity does not: those rows
become an unnamed former member.

## Before you press it

The screen shows what your account currently holds, so you are deciding with the
numbers in front of you:

- _You are in {n} groups._
- _You entered {n} expenses that will stay._
- _You are named in {n} settlements._
- _You still have an unsettled balance in {list}._

That last one is a nudge, not a block. You can delete an account that still owes
somebody money — but they will be looking at a balance against a former member,
so it is kinder to settle first.

## Doing it

- **Why are you leaving? (optional)** — _"It helps to know, and it is kept after
  your account is gone."_ Read that literally; see below.
- **Type DELETE to confirm** — the literal word, in capitals.
- **Delete my data**.

:::warning What you type here is kept

The answer is saved a moment before the account is deleted, and it survives the
deletion. What is saved is the text exactly as you typed it, up to 4,000
characters, together with the language and country that were on your account and
the date. The link back to your account is cleared, so the entry no longer says
who wrote it — but nothing in the text itself is removed or masked.

That matters because it is a free-text box, and people write their own name, or
an email address, or a phone number in it. Anything you put there stays there,
whether or not it identifies you.

The box is genuinely optional. **Leave it empty and nothing is written at all.**
If you do want to tell us why, it is worth saying it in a way you would be
content to have kept — the reason is the useful part, and your phone number
is not.

:::

When it is done: _Your data has been deleted_, and _"You are now a former member
of {n} groups."_

## If you just want to stop

Deleting is not the only exit, and it is the most final one.

- **Sign out** keeps everything: _"Nothing is deleted; sign back in whenever."_
  (Not for a guest account — see
  [Create your account](../getting-started/create-your-account.md#starting-without-an-account).)
- **Archive** a group to take it off your list without deleting anything.
- **Leave** a group to step out of it while it carries on — settle up first.
- Turn every notification off and simply stop opening the app. An account
  untouched for three years is deleted, and the personal data with it.
