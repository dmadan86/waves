Most of the people you split money with will not have Waves, and Waves does not
make that your problem. A name is enough.

## Somebody without the app

Type a name into **Add someone** and they exist in the group immediately, with
real balances against them:

> They do not need the app. Their share is recorded under their name, and if
> they join later with this email or number they claim everything already
> sitting there.

An email or phone number is optional, and only there so you can send them the
join link. The member list marks them **not joined yet**, and counts them:
_"{n} yet to join"_.

Their balance is not a placeholder even though they are:

> This person holds real balances. When they join, they can claim this history.

## Add a person, without a group

On **Friends**, **Add a person** records a one-off debt with no group to set up:

> Track what someone owes you — nobody needs the app, and no group to set up.

It asks for **Their name**, an **Amount**, and **Which way?** — **They owe me**
or **I owe them** — plus an optional note and how it was paid. Behind the scenes
it makes the smallest group it can and puts one expense in it, so the debt lives
in the same ledger as everything else and settles the same way.

## When they join

Somebody who joins through a group's invite link is offered the names already in
that group, and picks their own:

> Pick your name and everything already recorded for you comes with you.

Their past expenses and balances become theirs. Nothing is re-entered and no
balance changes.

## Merging duplicates

The same person added separately to three groups is three placeholders. Friends
notices — _"Seeing the same guest in more than one group? Merge the duplicates
into one person."_ — and **Merge people** combines them:

> Pick the guests who are the same person. Their balances are combined under one
> name.

Two things to know before you do it:

:::danger This cannot be undone

> Their separate balances are combined into one person for good. There's no way
> to split them back apart.

:::

- Only people **without** a Waves account can be merged, and only ones you share
  a group with. _"You can only merge guests you share a group with."_
- After a merge Waves offers to invite the person you have just assembled:
  _"Share a join link so they can see the groups you merged them into."_ It
  offers one link per group they now span.

**Assign to a contact** names a merge after somebody in your address book. It
only puts a name to them — it does not add them to any group.

## Finding somebody who does have Waves

**Find someone** searches by an exact email address or phone number:

> Type the exact email address or phone number they use on Waves.

There is no browsing and no search by name. If nothing comes back it is
deliberately ambiguous — _"Nobody uses that, or they have chosen not to be found
by it."_ — because a definite "no such person" would let somebody map the user
list. There is a daily limit on searches for the same reason.

What people can find you by is yours to decide; see
[How people find you](../privacy/discovery-settings.md).

## A person's profile

Tapping a person opens their profile: how many groups you have in common, every
group you share, and their contact details **if they have chosen to show them**.
Otherwise:

> {name} keeps their contact details to themselves.

For somebody who has not joined, there is simply nothing to show:

> Not on Waves yet, so there is nothing to show here.

**Gets paid at** shows their UPI ID or the equivalent, which is what makes
one-tap settling possible. **Remind** sends them a nudge about what they owe,
and the row then reads **Reminded** — see
[Settle up](./settle-up.md#reminding-somebody) for the daily limit.

## Admins

A group member with an account can be made an admin:

> Admins can edit the group, manage members, and set the overall budget.

A placeholder cannot: _"They have not joined yet. Only a member with an account
can be an admin."_
